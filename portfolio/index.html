CASE STUDY — 01

Identity & Access Management,
rebuilt for an org that couldn't afford downtime

01 — PROBLEM

Access requests at this scale weren't a UI problem — they were a trust problem. Provisioning took days, approvals lived in email threads, and every workaround engineers built became a security exception nobody tracked. The org wasn't asking for a prettier form. It was asking to stop being afraid of its own access system.

PLACEHOLDER — Legacy provisioning flow, annotated
02 — PROCESS

I spent the first three weeks refusing to open a design tool. Instead: shadowing approvers, mapping every exception path that had quietly become "normal," and building a single diagram of who could grant what to whom — a diagram that, it turned out, no one in the room had ever seen drawn in full.

PLACEHOLDER — Access-flow mapping session
03 — KEY DECISIONS

The biggest decision wasn't visual — it was to make risky requests slower on purpose, and safe ones instant. That asymmetry became the spine of the interface: a single status model, one approval surface instead of four, and language that told people exactly why a request was waiting.

PLACEHOLDER — Request status model, v3
04 — OUTCOME

Provisioning time dropped from days to under an hour for standard requests. More importantly, the exception queue — the thing everyone had quietly accepted as permanent — became small enough that security could actually review it. Nobody had to be brave about access anymore.
